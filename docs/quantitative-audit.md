# Quantitative audit and research contract

Audit date: 2026-09-09. Source baseline: commit `ee61c6a`; legacy line references below identify that commit, before this batch's additive code changes. This is a source and artifact inventory, not a rerun of reported experiments. No actual model pickle was loaded, no historical evaluator was run, no historical record was changed, and no broker was contacted.

## What can be concluded

ATLAS contains a substantial FX binary-options research pipeline, several reported chronological experiments, and evidence that executable opportunities differ sharply from attractive candle-only signals. It does **not** currently establish a reliable profitable strategy or superiority over matched simple controls. The checkout lacks the underlying candle database, prediction caches, run bundles and execution journals required to reproduce those claims. FX binary direction probabilities cannot support an equity or crypto return forecast without new domain-specific training and validation.

New baseline code in this batch is an offline research capability. Passing synthetic tests demonstrates software behavior, not investment performance. Existing H2/H3/H4 artifacts remain historical research artifacts; they are not silently upgraded to the corrected feature contract.

## Existing lifecycle

1. `collector.py:135–178` requests historical bars through the broker wrapper, normalizes them, reports gaps, validates OHLC/durations, and stores each collection as an immutable dataset. `storage.py:79–110` hashes canonical candle tuples. Canonical history merges snapshots and reports conflicts; `train.py:489–495` refuses unresolved conflicts, whereas `research_pooled.py:52` discards the report.
2. `features.py:247–258` splits missing-bar gaps before rolling indicators. `features.py:261–415` produces lagged returns, EMA/ATR/RSI, ADX, Bollinger/MACD, candle-shape, volume, multitimeframe, session and volatility-regime features. Backward as-of multitimeframe joins use completed groups (`113–139`). The target is later close versus current close or next open (`387–395`), with future-unavailable rows and ties marked missing.
3. `train.py:118–199` generates chronological calibration predictions, fits the calibrator on earlier out-of-fold outputs, and fits final base estimators only on their training window. Scaling lives inside the logistic pipeline. `train.py:298–397` performs outer walk-forward prediction and produces payout-gated binary signals. `ProbabilityAverager` (`58–97`) already supports a three-seed LightGBM plus logistic ensemble.
4. Immutable run bundles bind candles, signals, folds and the replay-engine binary by SHA-256 (`backtest.py:63–98`). Replay calls the external MIDAS executable; it is absent here. This is stronger provenance than a loose research cache, but no run bundle is present to verify.
5. Pooled/deep-history research variants (`research_pooled.py`, `research_wr.py`, `research_meta.py`, `research_era.py`, `research_best.py`, and related files) explore cross-asset predictors, tail thresholds and meta-models. Their multiple selections must be counted as screening, even where an output is named “holdout.” `experiments.py` and `registry.py` have separate append-only ledgers; variant accounting is not automatically enforced across all research scripts.
6. `live_model_build.py:29–60` builds H2/H4 from pooled labeled history and writes date-named pickle bundles with feature columns, asset list and training timestamps. `live_h2_runner.py:274–320` loads H2 plus optional H3/H4. The actual decision is H2's probability with deterministic EV/payout gates. H3/H4 observations are logged separately; the runner is not a weighted live ensemble. Model display count and execution influence are different things.
7. `analyzer.py:1–152` is a separate deterministic EURUSD EMA/RSI rule with freshness and payout gates. Its product/horizon and entry conditions differ from H2, so its outputs are not already a matched performance baseline.

Two source-only synthetic counterexamples were run without fitting a model:

- With 500 zero-volume bars, the old feature builder produced 186 rows. Appending a later nonzero-volume observation made **all 186 earlier rows disappear**. The v2 regression requires exact prefix equality of the feature columns and row availability; label columns are intentionally forward-looking and are excluded.
- With unchanged EURUSD/GBPUSD prices, changing only GBPUSD's missing future label to a known one changed EURUSD's legacy current `xs_quote_str` from `0.0` to `-0.2`. New assembly computes the basket first, then optionally filters target rows.

Additional limitations: `research_deephistory.py:31–51` skips unavailable downloaded years, and its duplicate handling does not establish conflict-free coverage. `research_universe_profile.py:77–81` uses `MAX(close)` over duplicate snapshots rather than canonical conflict resolution, and its volatility calculation includes across-gap changes as though they were equally spaced. `registry.py:31` does not reject negative trial counts, so the ledger is evidence of recorded trials rather than a guaranteed complete multiplicity denominator. None of these historical reports is regenerated in this batch.

The current evaluator **does** use `model_forward_start` and `purge=True` (`forward_eval.py:157–174,239`) to avoid using a model before its recorded training horizon. Older prose describing unconditional refusal is stale. That correction does not resolve the basket, payout and count discrepancies above. A CLI description of “one-time” evaluation is not a durable execution receipt: `forward_eval.py:602–663` allows reruns over mutable inputs, with no pinned terminal observation window.

## Baseline status and defensible ensemble design

No matched, cost-aware buy-and-hold/random/momentum/mean-reversion performance artifact is present. The new `atlas.analytics.compare_baselines` supplies that **capability**, but only synthetic fixtures were run here. No market dataset comparison or winner was produced.

The existing research already tested a three-seed LightGBM/logistic average and reported no consistent win-rate gain: 56.6/57.3/59.0 versus 56.9/57.6/58.6 (`FORWARD_TEST.md:201–206`). Adding model names or averaging unrelated scores is not evidence of improvement.

A defensible next experiment would compare H2, logistic regression and a fixed equal-weight probability pool only when they predict the **same outcome, horizon, entry convention and as-of observation**. Evaluate them on identical eligible timestamps, with a constant probability/base-rate control, no-trade/cash, buy-and-hold where the product supports it, seeded random entries, momentum and mean-reversion controls. Binary options and equity buy-and-hold require different payoff engines; do not compare their raw win rates as an investment ranking.

If weights or a stacker are learned, train them only on earlier genuinely out-of-fold base predictions, with nested chronological purges and a later locked final test. Recalibration is an empirical requirement, not an assumed property of an average. H3 predicts conditional correctness of a selected H2 action; H2 predicts direction. These are different random variables and must not be averaged as identical probabilities. Regime weights, Bayesian model averaging and research-driven probability adjustments are deferred until an adequate time-aligned prediction matrix and independent evidence exist.

Primary methodological sources checked on 2026-09-09:

- [scikit-learn TimeSeriesSplit](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit.html): chronological ordering and the sample-count meaning of `gap` matter; equal spacing is relevant to comparable fold durations. ATLAS's timestamp and label-horizon checks are application-specific safeguards inferred from this contract.
- [scikit-learn StackingClassifier](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.StackingClassifier.html): the final estimator uses cross-validated predictions; fitting a stacker on base-model training predictions risks overfitting. Default classifier folds do not supply ATLAS's required time purge.
- [scikit-learn VotingClassifier](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.VotingClassifier.html): soft voting combines probabilities and is intended for well-calibrated classifiers. That recommendation is not a guarantee that the resulting pool stays calibrated.
- [Ranjan and Gneiting, Combining Probability Forecasts (2010)](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/72/1/71/7076442): probability pooling can lose calibration even when constituents are calibrated. The paper motivates evaluating calibration and proper scores of the combined predictor itself.

## Implemented boundaries and APIs

- `features.build_features(..., feature_version="2.0.0")` is explicit new research. Default `FEATURE_VERSION` stays `1.3.0`. The new volume calculation cannot relabel or overwrite a frozen model. `atlas.research_features.build_research_pool` validates candles, assembles current observed FX feature rows, then permits an optional final label filter. It does not run training, load a database or broker, or rewrite a cache.
- `train.walk_forward(..., feature_version="2.0.0")` requires a matching frame version, validates chronological/gap contracts before fitting, and records the new feature version and hash in both manifest and signals. CLI `--feature-version` remains opt-in. Legacy inference/builders continue to use their original default. No market-data training job or historical artifact rebuild occurred; estimator regression tests use only synthetic fixtures.
- `StrategyRegistry.analyze(symbol, asset_class, bars, now=...)` returns independent causal momentum and mean-reversion records: strategy/version, stable content hash, direction, price-distance score with units, horizon, as-of timestamp, reasons and limitations. Confidence, probability and expected return stay null. The signal ID excludes the request clock, so requesting the same observation later cannot mint a new actionable identity. `set_enabled` disables one rule independently; there is no numerical fusion.
- Registry rules are **research-only**, even if a newly registered object asserts that it is validated. This extension boundary intentionally does not promote descriptor metadata into live permission. A separately vetted server SignalProvider must bind a calibrated artifact digest, training domain, target/horizon, approval policy and evaluation evidence before supplying execution-eligible signals. An MCP caller cannot register an arbitrary Python strategy or provide its own probability as validation.
- `performance_report` derives descriptive metrics from explicitly net trades or complete gross/cost records. Missing costs are not assumed zero. Drawdown requires an equity path; annualized return, volatility, Sharpe and Sortino require sufficient equally spaced returns and an explicit annualization convention. Calibration/correlation remain null when their required inputs are absent. Thirty observations is only a reporting floor, not a statistical-power guarantee.
- `compare_baselines` requires explicit fee/slippage assumptions. It uses causal prior-close decisions, next observed opens, long/cash positions and final-close liquidation over one matched out-of-sample window. Buy-and-hold pays one entry/exit; random is a reproducible single seeded control, not a significance distribution. Expanding training ranges are disclosed context windows: no estimator is fitted or tuned. Output cannot be relabeled paper, live or untouched holdout. Exposure and return correlations are descriptive, not a justification for weighting.

The baseline simulator does not model dividends, splits, borrow, depth, impact or venue sessions; adjustment and execution feasibility must be supplied by a future product-specific dataset and engine. Input timestamps identify closed bars, and fill records identify the bar containing the assumed fill rather than inventing exact exchange execution timestamps. Signals do not infer corporate-action adjustments, provider authenticity or market hours from a candle's presence.

## Financial arithmetic and operational reporting

Platform financial inputs are finite Decimals with at most 64 significant digits and at most 36 integer and 36 fractional decimal places. Inputs exceeding this contract are rejected, not rounded into eligibility. Risk checks, sizing, paper accounting and crypto adapter arithmetic run inside a fresh 256-digit Decimal context with explicit rounding/traps, independent of a caller's or library's ambient thread context. Ordinary monetary strings remain plain decimal. A regression demonstrates that an order worth 100.10 remains above a 100.00 cap even when the caller uses two-digit precision.

The paper simulator books derived values at 36 fractional places using half-even rounding; pro-rata cost allocation is an accounting estimate at that declared precision. Estimated per-unit fees round upwards so the risk gate does not underestimate them. This is not a claim about any broker's tick size or currency minor unit. Exact broker prices and quantities are never silently quantized by this helper. Paper cash, fees, partial average-cost allocation and restart recovery are exercised with synthetic fixtures under changed caller precision.

Operational performance reports separate fees on terminal sell executions from acknowledged fees across all order intents, including buys. The latter remains unknown if any fee record is missing, with a separately labeled known subtotal. Realized net P&L relies on the adapter's allocated-cost-basis accounting and is not reduced by these fees again. Canceled orders with a positive partial sell fill remain in performance. Pending inventory is not a completed profitable trade. Operational windows use recent intent creation or journal update timestamps; these are explicitly **not broker fill dates**. Account return, turnover, regime results, probability buckets, calibration and live-versus-backtest divergence remain unavailable without their required aligned data.
